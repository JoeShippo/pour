# Why Your PDF Menu Is Costing You Customers

Picture it. Saturday afternoon, someone's deciding where to go for food. They find your pub, tap "Menu", and…

A PDF starts downloading. On one bar of signal. When it finally opens, it's a beautifully designed A3 page, shrunk to fit a phone screen. They pinch. They zoom. They scroll sideways. They give up and go to the place that showed them a menu straight away.

PDF menus are everywhere in hospitality, and we get why. Your designer made a lovely one for the tables, so why not stick it online? But on a website, that PDF quietly works against you. Here's why.

## 1. They're painful on a phone

Most people looking at your menu online are on their phone. PDFs are designed for paper, not small screens. They:

- Take longer to load, especially with big images or fancy fonts.
- Often open in a separate app or download to the phone.
- Need pinching and zooming to read.
- Don't adapt to the screen size.

Every extra second and every extra pinch is a chance for someone to give up.

## 2. Search engines see less of them

Google *can* read text in PDFs, but it's not the same as a proper web page. A PDF menu usually:

- Isn't connected to the rest of your site in a way that helps it rank.
- Doesn't show up nicely in search results.
- Has no structured data telling Google "this is a menu, here are the dishes, here are the prices".
- Is often an image of text, which search engines can barely read at all.

That matters when someone searches "Sunday roast near me" or "pub with vegan food in [town]". If your roast is hidden inside a PDF, you're less likely to show up.

## 3. AI search can't easily use them

More people are now asking ChatGPT, Google's AI Overviews or Gemini questions like "Where can I get a good gluten-free Sunday lunch near me?" These tools build their answers from what they can read and understand online.

A clear web page that says "Gluten-free roast available every Sunday, 12–5pm" is easy for them to use. The same information buried in a designed PDF often isn't. (More on this in *Will ChatGPT recommend your pub?*)

## 4. They're out of date the moment you change anything

Menus change. Specials, seasonal dishes, prices, the guest ale, the pie that sold out. With a PDF, every change means:

1. Ask the designer to update it (or wrestle with the file yourself).
2. Export a new PDF.
3. Upload it to the website.
4. Remember to delete the old one.
5. Update the link on your Google Business Profile.

So it doesn't get done, and people turn up expecting a dish you stopped serving in March.

## 5. They're not accessible

Many PDFs aren't readable by screen readers, which blind and partially sighted people use to browse the web. Text-as-image PDFs are the worst for this. A proper web menu can be read aloud, resized and navigated by keyboard. It's the right thing to do, and it's more customers.

## 6. They hide your allergen info

Allergen information is something people actively look for, often before they've even decided to visit. On a web page, you can make dietary info clear, searchable and filterable (vegan, vegetarian, gluten-free). In a PDF it's usually a tiny footnote.

## What to do instead

Put your menu on a proper web page. It doesn't need to be fancy.

**A good web menu:**

- Loads instantly on a phone.
- Uses real text, not images of text.
- Is organised into clear sections (starters, mains, Sunday roasts, desserts, kids).
- Shows prices.
- Marks dietary options clearly (V, VG, GF) with allergen info available.
- Has a "last updated" date so people trust it.
- Can be updated by your team in a couple of minutes, from a phone if needed.
- Uses structured data so search engines know it's a menu.
- Is linked directly from your Google Business Profile.

**Bonus points:**

- A separate page for each menu (lunch, dinner, Sunday, drinks).
- Your drinks list or tap list on the same site, ideally updating automatically from Untappd for Business or your EPOS.
- A button to book a table right below the menu.

## Keep the PDF, just don't rely on it

There's nothing wrong with *also* offering a downloadable PDF for people who want to print it, like groups planning a party. Just make it the backup, not the main event.

## Quick test

Grab your phone, turn off Wi-Fi, and go to your website. Time how long it takes to see your menu and read the first dish. If it's more than a few seconds, or you had to zoom, your customers are having the same experience.
