# What Is a Headless Website, and Does Your Brewery Need One?

If you've talked to a web agency lately, someone might have said the word "headless". It sounds like a horror film or a very strong IPA. It's neither.

Here's what it means in plain English, when it's worth it, and why for most independent breweries and pubs the honest answer is "not yet".

## The pub analogy

Think of a traditional website like a pub where the kitchen and the bar are the same room. The chef cooks, then walks the plate straight to the table. Simple, works well, and most pubs run perfectly happily like that.

A **headless** website splits the kitchen from the front of house. The kitchen (where your content and products live) just prepares the food. The front of house (what visitors actually see) is a completely separate operation that collects plates from the kitchen and serves them however it likes.

In website terms:

- The **back end** (the "body") is where you manage content and products. That might be WordPress, Shopify or a dedicated content system.
- The **front end** (the "head") is the website people see, built separately with modern web tools.
- They talk to each other through an API, which is basically the hatch between kitchen and bar.

"Headless" just means the back end doesn't come with its own front end attached.

## Why would anyone do that?

Because once the kitchen and the front of house are separate, each can be really good at its job.

**The good bits:**

- **Speed.** Headless sites can be extremely fast, which is great for phones on patchy signal and helps with search rankings.
- **Freedom.** The design isn't limited by a theme or template. If you can imagine it, it can be built.
- **Best of both worlds.** You can use Shopify's brilliant checkout for your beer shop *and* a proper content system for your stories, events and tap lists, all on one seamless site.
- **One kitchen, many fronts.** The same content can feed your website, an app, screens in the taproom or a tap list board.
- **Security.** With the back end tucked away, there's less for attackers to poke at.

## The catch

**The not-so-good bits:**

- **Cost.** A headless build takes more development work, so it costs more upfront.
- **Ongoing developer time.** Changes to layout or new features usually need a developer. Adding a blog post is easy. Adding a new kind of page often isn't.
- **More moving parts.** Two systems plus the bit connecting them means more to host, monitor and keep updated.
- **Fewer plug-and-play extras.** Lots of WordPress plugins and Shopify apps assume a traditional set-up. Some won't work headless without extra work.

## So, does your brewery need one?

Probably not right now. A headless site makes sense when **most** of these are true:

- You're doing serious volume through your online shop *and* publishing lots of content.
- You've hit real limits with a traditional WordPress or Shopify site (speed, design or integrations).
- You've got multiple sites, brands or channels to feed from one place.
- You've got the budget for a bigger build and ongoing developer support.
- Your website is central to how the business grows, not just a shop window.

If you're a single taproom, a pub, or a brewery with a small webshop, a well-built WordPress or Shopify site will be fast enough, flexible enough and much easier on the wallet.

## A quick decision guide

| Your situation | What we'd usually suggest |
|---|---|
| Single pub or taproom, events and menus | WordPress |
| Brewery with a busy online shop, light content | Shopify |
| Brewery with a moderate shop and lots of content | WordPress + WooCommerce |
| Growing brewery, big shop *and* big content, multiple brands or sites | Headless |

## What headless looks like in practice

A typical headless brewery set-up might be:

- **Shopify** handling products, stock, checkout and subscriptions.
- **WordPress** (or another content system) handling brewery stories, events, blog posts and taproom info.
- **A modern front end** pulling both together into one fast, custom-designed website.

Customers never see the join. They just get a fast site where they can read about the new release, check what's on tap and buy a mixed case in one place.
